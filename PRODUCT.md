# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary user is a hired cashier in a small Mozambican retail shop or pharmacy — possibly with low tech literacy, using the app under pressure during a busy sale. Secondary user is the shop owner, who checks reports, manages inventory, and monitors the system. Both operate on modest hardware (low-cost Android tablets or cheap laptops) under bright shop lighting.

## Product Purpose

Vela is a point-of-sale system built for small businesses in Mozambique. It handles sales (barcode scan + cart), cash and M-Pesa payments, inventory management, and sales reports. Success means a cashier can complete a sale in under 15 seconds without confusion, and an owner can trust the numbers in the reports module.

## Brand Personality

Calm · Simple · Trustworthy. Vela — the sail, the candle — should feel like a tool made specifically for this market, not a generic SaaS product that was localized. Approachable enough for a first-day cashier, reliable enough for an owner staking their business on it. The maritime blue palette is intentional: a single saturated, deep blue (not the iOS-cobalt SaaS reflex), with cool neutrals tinted toward the same hue.

## Anti-references

- Tailwind-template SaaS dashboards: gradient hero metrics, decorative icons everywhere, the iOS-blue-on-flat-card look.
- Busy enterprise retail software: packed toolbars, Windows-era table UIs, feature overload.
- Overly minimal / cold: sterile whitespace, prototype feel, no personality.

## Design Principles

1. **Speed over features at the till.** The POS screen is the cashier's world. Every interaction must be obvious and fast — no hunting, no hesitation.
2. **Numbers are the UI.** Prices, quantities, totals: make them impossible to misread. Size, weight, and contrast do more than icons or labels.
3. **Calm without dullness.** Restraint isn't blandness. Vela earns interest through type and rhythm — not through decorative clutter.
4. **Built for the screen in front of them.** Cheap laptop screens, bright fluorescent lighting, slightly dirty touchscreens. Design for this, not for a Retina display in a design studio.
5. **Trust through clarity.** Owners must trust the reports. Cashiers must trust the total. Every number shown must feel authoritative — no ambiguity about what it represents.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

High contrast is the primary constraint — cheap screens and bright shop lighting demand strong text/background separation. WCAG AA minimum throughout. Keyboard-first for barcode scanner workflows; touch-friendly tap targets (≥44px) for tablet use.
